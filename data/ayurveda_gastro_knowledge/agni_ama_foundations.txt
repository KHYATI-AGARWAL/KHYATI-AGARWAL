In Ayurvedic gastroenterology, agni (digestive-metabolic fire) is central to appetite, digestion,
and tissue nourishment. Mandagni is associated with heaviness, bloating, lethargy, and incomplete
digestion. Vishamagni is irregular and often linked to fluctuating appetite, gas, and alternating
bowel patterns. Tikshnagni may manifest as excessive hunger, burning, loose stools, and pitta signs.

Ama is described as incompletely transformed metabolic residue that can obstruct channels and trigger
inflammation-like symptom clusters. In clinical translation, ama-oriented patterns often overlap with
post-prandial heaviness, coated tongue, foul belching, constipation with mucus, or mixed IBS features.
Initial management prioritizes langhana (lightening), dipana-pachana (kindling and digesting), and
structured meal timing before stronger rejuvenative strategies.
